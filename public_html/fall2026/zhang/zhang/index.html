Garden Variety War

by Elena Zhang

In this movie, the soldier comes home with a sack full of weapons. The soldier is my father. He leaves the sack of weapons at my feet, then disappears into a movie. I take the weapons out of the sack and bury them in the backyard garden. I make a tombstone out of cardboard. It says, here lies my pet parakeet, may she rest in peace. She was only three years old, same as me. That night, the weapons grow into weapon trees. We live off of the harvest for the rest of our lives.

Stratification

by Elena Zhang

There is an underwater elevator in the middle of the lake. You pay two memories to open the doors. Inside, the walls are covered with mirrors. See, there is you and you and you and you and her. There are no buttons. Only she knows where you must go. As the elevator glides down, you look through a tiny window in the floor and watch ghosts pulse in the dark. Glowing, and immortal. Streams of hair swirl slowly like tentacles. Familiar eyes blink at you through a glassy film. You saw them just yesterday afternoon. An argument filters out between strains of background music. The elevator shudders to a stop. The doors open. Take a breath. This is what you’ve always dreamed of.
