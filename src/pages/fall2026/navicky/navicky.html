On Walmart’s 1996 Bid to Erect a Superstore Atop George Washington’s Childhood Home

by Jefferson Navicky

The air flapped the side of my face. Properly caffeinated, I made my way towards the gates of heaven. The automatic doors smoothly slide open to welcome me a good five feet before I am actually in the doorway, which satisfies me in ways I don’t always like to admit.

If only everything could predict my desires as generously, as simply, as smoothly. What do I want? To walk forward and for the doors to the future to open so that I do not have to slow my roll for the doors’ automatic ocean whose whoosh I ride to find the plaques: George Washington slept here. And here. And here too. George Washington ate a pork chop here. Abandoned his dog here. Told a young soldier to go fuck himself over there in that field. Serviced himself in a tent that might as well have been a canvas castle and we believe it atop that hill. George’s shadow forsook him here. Felt bum. He stepped in horse shit thereabouts, plaqued and historically marked. He didn’t think he’d make it to old age right over there. And that’s where he wore empire, and that’s where he ate off empire, drank it and slept it, and effortlessly ingested it. He lost his temper and lashed out below that cherry tree that later somebody chopped down, but Washington got the credit, not like he needed it.

If words spoken against Washington are words spoken against America, then in some field of asters beyond Household Appliances, I find the godless coin, Washington’s face on it, and I bring it to Customer Service for authentication where they offer to take it off my hands, keep it safe, but do they think I’m a fool, that I don’t know my Washingtonology? I plan to use it as a chip in that huge red, white, and blue vending machine at the back of the store with all the good fluffy stuff inside, the one that has stolen all of my previous coins, but not this time, this time with the help of old dollar-face George, I’m finally going to get what I want.

Jefferson Navicky is the author of Head of Island Beautification for the Rural Outlands, as well as Antique Densities, which won the 2022 Maine Literary Award for Poetry. He lives in Midcoast Maine.
