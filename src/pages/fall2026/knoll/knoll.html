A Suffragette Kisses Harry Houdini

by Tricia Knoll

What nested in his wife’s kiss on stage on March 17, 1904 that made the strong man blush? Some said Bess passed the key to the handcuff that bound him. Tipped it to his tongue. Others thought she was one of many suffragettes he dared to tie him up in knots to help publicize their work. Could he foresee, that man who stared through ice, that women would get the right to vote one day but not for sixteen years after the famous kiss. Was the kiss of suffrage so soft, he ached for more muscle, not knowing that orbicularis oris, sphincter of telling, was also Mona Lisa's smile and the determined press of lips on the marching women’s faces. Those who sang “Awake, awake, ye sisters all.”

The Interview

by Tricia Knoll

I tell the newspaper editor I have a great idea for a position with his paper. I write features based on loss. I wear my “Women with a Voice” t-shirt. I am a professional mourner and list my certifications in Moirology.com. Cite teenage volunteer work cleaning veterans’ headstones and mowing graveyard lawns on Memorial Day Weekends. He shrugs; he needs a smoke and asks me to step outside. He leans against the building tiles without a jacket despite the cold and asks me to explain. I’ve written obituaries for a newspaper whose demographics are much like that of his small Vermont town that boasts one Mexican restaurant, a gas station with an attached convenience store, a knit shop, two police officers, and a pit bull rescue center. I can mourn up a storm, attend funerals or memorials where no one else shows up. I have two drawers of ironed handkerchiefs and costumes for fictive aunts and sisters. I can sound sadder than an out-of-tune viola. He says no. I touch his wrist and explain what is missing in his town: passenger pigeons, caribou, elk. The catamount, for Christ’s sake. Sea minks. I can help his readers feel the death of ash trees. Elms. Chestnuts. The closing of the elementary school and the social security office in the neighboring town. Canceled government services – grants to volunteer firefighters and libraries. Food aid. I remind him loss repeats itself, faster than ever. If he doesn’t hire me, his readers will forget and forgetting is death. He responds: Come inside. You can work for free.
